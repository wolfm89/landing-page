5. Train Price Monitor

Train Price Monitor is a free, non-commercial web app that lets you watch train connections and get notified when the price rises above a limit you choose.

Hosting. The app runs on Amazon Web Services EMEA SARL, 38 Avenue John F. Kennedy, L-1855 Luxembourg (“AWS”), which acts as my processor. The app’s data is stored in the AWS Frankfurt am Main region (eu-central-1). The app’s files are delivered by Amazon CloudFront, which processes your IP address to do so; I do not store CloudFront access logs.

Account. You need an account to use the app. When you sign up, I process your email address, your name and your password. Sign-up and sign-in run on Amazon Cognito; the password is not stored in plain text. To confirm your email address and to reset your password, Amazon Cognito sends you an email through Amazon SES from trainpricemonitor@wolfgangmoser.eu. I also store your email address, your name, a randomly generated user ID and your settings in the app’s database (Amazon DynamoDB).

Profile and travel preferences. You can optionally upload a profile picture (JPEG, PNG or GIF up to 500 KB) and save travel preferences: discount cards such as the BahnCard, your age group and whether you have a Deutschlandticket. The profile picture is stored encrypted in Amazon S3 and shown only to you through links that expire after one hour.

Connection search. When you search for stations, the app matches your input against a station list built into the app; no data is sent to third parties. To search for connections and prices, the app queries the timetable and fare service of bahn.de operated by DB Fernverkehr AG, Europa-Allee 78–84, 60486 Frankfurt am Main, Germany. Only the search details are sent: departure and arrival station, date and time, travel class, search options such as means of transport and changes, and, where applicable, bicycle, age group, discount card and Deutschlandticket. The requests are made by the app’s servers at AWS; your IP address, name, email address and user ID are not sent. DB processes the requests under its own responsibility.

Forwarding through Decodo. If a request from the app does not get through to bahn.de, for example because bahn.de rejects it or does not answer in time, the same request is sent to bahn.de through the “Decodo” service of UAB “Data troops”, Švitrigailos str. 34, LT-03230 Vilnius, Lithuania; at times, all requests may take this route. Decodo acts as my processor, receives the same search details, again without any information about you, and forwards the request through a worldwide network of proxy servers.

Watched connections and notifications. When you watch a connection, I store the search details, your price limit, the expiry time (one hour before departure) and the most recent price with the journey details. The app checks prices every hour. If a price reaches or exceeds your limit, a connection expires or can no longer be tracked, you get a notification in the app. If email notifications are switched on (the default; you can switch them off in your profile), I also send you an email through Amazon SES from trainpricemonitor@wolfgangmoser.eu. It contains your first name, the stations, for price alerts the current price and your limit, and a link to the app. If an email to you bounces permanently or is reported as spam, SES puts your address on a suppression list so that no further emails are sent to it.

Legal basis. The processing described above is necessary to provide the app you want to use by signing up (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). This also applies to the optional details in your profile. If you open the app without an account, the legal basis for delivering it through Amazon CloudFront is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is secure and reliable delivery of the app.

Logs. To find errors and prevent abuse, the app’s servers log their operations in Amazon CloudWatch. The logs contain timestamps, the type and outcome of requests and pseudonymous identifiers (user, connection and notification IDs), but no IP addresses, names, email addresses or search terms. Separately, AWS records calls to its programming interfaces in the AWS CloudTrail event history: every request your browser makes to Amazon Cognito (such as sign-up, sign-in, renewing the sign-in tokens, changing the password, deleting the account) with the IP address, the browser identifier and the Cognito ID of your account. Where your email address was registered with or removed from SES while it ran in restricted mode, the event history keeps that for 90 days as well. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; my legitimate interest is the secure and error-free operation of the app.

10. Retention

  • Landing page and blog: I do not store any data about your visit. GitHub decides how long it keeps its security logs (section 3).
  • Account: until you delete your account or ask me to. Sign-ups that you do not confirm within seven days are deleted automatically. Deleting the account immediately removes the profile picture, watched connections, notifications and, if present, a suppression-list entry at SES as well. Deleted database entries remain in the database backup for up to 35 more days; the backup is only used to recover from an incident.
  • Watched connections: until you delete them, otherwise until the first hourly check after they expire; if that check does not happen, seven days after expiry.
  • Notifications: 90 days after they are created or, if later, seven days after the related connection expires. Price alerts are deleted together with the expired connection; deleting a connection also deletes its notifications.

  • Logs: 14 days; AWS CloudTrail event history: 90 days.
  • Local storage: until you delete the entries or sign out (section 7).
  • Emails: see section 6.

The database carries out these time-based deletions in the background, usually within a few days of the time stated.
